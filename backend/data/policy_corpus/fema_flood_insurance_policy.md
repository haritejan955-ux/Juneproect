# FEMA National Flood Insurance Program — Standard Flood Insurance Policy

Source: Federal Emergency Management Agency, National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), Standard
Flood Insurance Policy. fema.gov. This file is a representative, educational summary of
well-established public provisions for use in a demo claims pipeline — it is not verbatim policy
text and must not be used as a substitute for an actual NFIP policy or for legal advice.

7.1 Coverage Structure

The Standard Flood Insurance Policy provides two separate limits of coverage: Building Property
coverage and Personal Property (contents) coverage. Each is subject to its own deductible and
policy limit; a loss to contents does not draw against the building coverage limit, and vice
versa. For a single-family residential structure, standard maximum limits are $250,000 for
building coverage and $100,000 for contents coverage.

7.2 Waiting Period

Except in specified circumstances (such as a policy required in connection with the making,
increasing, extending, or renewing of a loan), a new flood insurance policy does not take effect
until 30 calendar days after the application date and premium payment.

7.3 Proof of Loss Requirement

The policyholder must file a signed, sworn Proof of Loss within 60 days after the loss, unless
that deadline is extended by FEMA for a specific declared disaster. The Proof of Loss must
itemize the damaged property, the replacement or actual cash value as applicable, and the amount
claimed. A claim filed without a timely Proof of Loss may be denied for that reason
independent of whether the underlying flood damage is otherwise covered.

7.4 Valuation Basis

Coverage for a single-family, principal-residence building is provided on a replacement-cost
basis, provided the building is insured to at least 80% of its full replacement cost at the time
of loss; otherwise, a coinsurance penalty applies and reduces the claim payment proportionally.
Contents coverage and coverage for non-principal-residence buildings is provided on an actual
cash value basis (replacement cost less physical depreciation).

7.5 Common Exclusions

The Standard Flood Insurance Policy excludes, among other things: property and belongings
outside of an insured building; most self-propelled vehicles including their parts; damage
caused directly by earth movement even if the earth movement is caused by flooding; and living
expenses, loss of income, or additional living expenses incurred while the insured building is
unable to be occupied.
