# FEHB Plan Brochures (Sample Structure)

Source: US Office of Personnel Management, Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB) Program
plan brochures. opm.gov. This file is a representative, educational summary of the standard
brochure structure and common provisions used across FEHB plans, for use in a demo claims
pipeline — it is not a verbatim brochure for any specific plan and must not be used as a
substitute for an actual FEHB plan brochure or for legal advice.

8.1 Plan Brochure Structure

Every FEHB plan brochure, as approved annually by OPM, follows a standard section structure:
Section 1 (facts about the plan), Section 2 (changes for the new plan year), Section 3 (how the
plan works, including definitions of in-network and out-of-network care), Section 4 (your costs
for covered services, including deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance), Section 5 (covered
services organized by category), Section 6 (exclusions — services and supplies the plan does not
cover), Section 9 (coordination of benefits with other coverage, including Medicare), and
Section 10 (definitions of terms used in the brochure).

8.2 Covered Services Categories (Section 5)

FEHB plans must describe covered services within defined categories, typically including:
medical and surgical services; hospital coverage; emergency services and urgently needed care;
mental health and substance use disorder services; prescription drug coverage; and other
covered services such as durable medical equipment and home health care. Each category
description states applicable deductibles, copayments, and any prior-authorization requirements.

8.3 Coordination of Benefits (Section 9)

When a covered individual has coverage under more than one plan (for example, FEHB and Medicare
Part B for a retiree), the plans coordinate benefits according to an order of determination.
FEHB plans generally pay first for active employees and their family members and pay second,
supplementing Medicare, for retirees enrolled in both.

8.4 Exclusions (Section 6)

Common FEHB exclusions include services not medically necessary, cosmetic surgery except to
correct an accidental injury or congenital defect, custodial care, and services for which no
charge would be made in the absence of insurance. A denial based on an exclusion must identify
the specific exclusion provision relied upon.

8.5 Disputed Claims Process

A member who disagrees with a plan's initial claim decision may request reconsideration directly
from the plan. If the member remains dissatisfied after the plan's reconsideration, the member
may ask OPM to review the disputed claim; OPM's review is based on the documentation in the
disputed-claims file assembled by the plan and the member.
