# California Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations — 10 CCR § 2695

Source: California Code of Regulations, Title 10, Chapter 5, Subchapter 7.5 (§ 2695.1 et seq.);
insurance.ca.gov. This file is a representative, educational summary of well-established public
provisions for use in a demo claims pipeline — it is not verbatim regulatory text and must not
be used as a substitute for the official regulations or for legal advice.

6.1 Acknowledgment of Claim (§ 2695.5)

Every insurer must acknowledge receipt of a claim within 15 calendar days. If the acknowledgment
is not in writing, the insurer must keep a record of the date, method, and content of the
acknowledgment. All relevant claim forms and instructions necessary for the claimant to comply
with the insurer's requirements must be provided within the same 15-day period.

6.2 Timely Investigation (§ 2695.5(e))

An insurer must begin investigation of a claim within 15 calendar days of receiving notice.
Investigations must be conducted in a manner that does not unreasonably delay the processing of
the claim.

6.3 Acceptance or Denial Within 40 Days (§ 2695.7)

Upon receiving all information necessary to determine whether to accept or deny a claim, an
insurer must do so within 40 calendar days. If more time is needed, the insurer must provide the
claimant a written status update no less than every 30 calendar days, stating the reasons more
time is needed and what, if anything, is required of the claimant.

6.4 Standards for Denial Letters (§ 2695.7(b))

Every claim denial, or partial denial, must be communicated in writing and must state the
specific policy provisions, conditions, or exclusions relied upon. Where the denial is based
upon an application of specific policy language to particular facts, the denial letter must
include reference to the facts and the policy provision, condition, or exclusion.

6.5 Prohibition on Unnecessary Documentation Demands (§ 2695.7(c))

An insurer may not persist in seeking information not reasonably required for or material to the
resolution of a claim dispute. Requiring a claimant to resubmit documentation already provided,
without a specific reason the prior submission was insufficient, violates this standard.

6.6 Fair Claims Settlement — Reasonable Investigation of Fraud Indicators

Where an insurer identifies indicators of potential fraud (for example, duplicate billing for
the same date of service, or a diagnosis inconsistent with the billed procedure), the insurer
must document the specific indicator and the evidence supporting it before adjusting or denying
the claim on that basis; a bare assertion of "suspected fraud" without supporting evidence does
not satisfy the investigation standard.
