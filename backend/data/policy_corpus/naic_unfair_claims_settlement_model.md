# NAIC Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Model Act

Source: National Association of Insurance Commissioners, Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Model
Act and Model Regulation. naic.org. This file is a representative, educational summary of
provisions widely adopted (with state-specific variation) across US jurisdictions, for use in a
demo claims pipeline — it is not verbatim regulatory text and must not be used as a substitute
for the official model act, a specific state's adopted version, or legal advice.

5.1 Prompt Acknowledgment of Claims

An insurer must acknowledge receipt of a claim within a reasonable time, and must respond to
claim-related communications from a claimant within 15 working days. Failure to acknowledge and
begin processing a claim promptly is itself an unfair claims settlement practice, independent of
the ultimate coverage determination.

5.2 Reasonable Investigation Standard

An insurer must adopt and implement reasonable standards for the prompt investigation of claims.
Refusing to pay a claim without conducting a reasonable investigation based on all available
information is a prohibited practice. An investigation is not reasonable if it relies on
selectively reading only the portions of submitted documentation that support denial while
disregarding contrary evidence in the same file.

5.3 Timely Affirmation or Denial

Insurers must affirm or deny coverage of a claim within a reasonable time after proof-of-loss
documentation has been completed and submitted. A denial that is unreasonably delayed, or that
is issued without the claimant having been given a meaningful opportunity to supply missing
documentation, is a prohibited practice.

5.4 Good Faith Settlement

Insurers must attempt in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair, and equitable settlement of
claims in which liability has become reasonably clear. Compelling claimants to institute
litigation to recover amounts due, by offering substantially less than the amount ultimately
recovered, is a prohibited practice.

5.5 Basis for Denial Must Be Documented

Any claim denial, in whole or in part, must be accompanied by a written explanation of the
specific policy provision, condition, or exclusion on which the denial is based. A denial letter
that cites no specific clause, or that cites a clause not actually present in the claimant's
policy, does not satisfy this standard.

5.6 Prohibited Practices Summary

Prohibited practices under the Model Act include: misrepresenting relevant policy provisions;
failing to promptly provide a reasonable explanation for a denial; failing to affirm or deny
coverage within a reasonable time; and unreasonably delaying investigation or payment of a claim
by requiring the claimant to submit a preliminary claim report and then requiring subsequent
submission of essentially duplicate reports.
