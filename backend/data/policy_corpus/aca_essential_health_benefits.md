# ACA Essential Health Benefits

Source: Affordable Care Act, 42 U.S.C. § 18022; healthcare.gov. This file is a representative,
educational summary of the ten statutory essential health benefit (EHB) categories for use in a
demo claims pipeline — it is not verbatim regulatory text and must not be used as a substitute
for the official statute/regulations or for legal advice.

4.1 The Ten Essential Health Benefit Categories

Non-grandfathered individual and small-group health plans must cover items and services in each
of the following ten categories, at a minimum:

  (1) Ambulatory patient services (outpatient care without hospital admission)
  (2) Emergency services
  (3) Hospitalization
  (4) Maternity and newborn care
  (5) Mental health and substance use disorder services, including behavioral health treatment
  (6) Prescription drugs
  (7) Rehabilitative and habilitative services and devices
  (8) Laboratory services
  (9) Preventive and wellness services and chronic disease management
  (10) Pediatric services, including oral and vision care

4.2 EHB as a Coverage Floor

The essential health benefit categories function as a floor, not a ceiling: a state benchmark
plan or issuer may offer coverage more generous than the EHB categories, but may not exclude
coverage for a service that falls within an EHB category solely on the basis of policy language
electing not to cover it. A denial that would otherwise be permitted by policy language must
still be evaluated against whether the underlying service falls within an EHB category.

4.3 Mental Health Parity

Essential health benefit plans that cover mental health and substance use disorder services must
do so at parity with medical and surgical benefits — financial requirements (copays,
coinsurance) and treatment limitations (visit limits, prior authorization) applied to mental
health and substance use disorder benefits may not be more restrictive than those applied to
substantially all medical and surgical benefits in the same classification.

4.4 Rehabilitative and Habilitative Services

Coverage must include both rehabilitative services (restoring a function lost due to illness or
injury, e.g. physical therapy following surgery) and habilitative services (developing a
function never possessed, e.g. pediatric speech therapy for a developmental delay). A plan may
not deny coverage for a habilitative service solely because the underlying condition is
long-term or chronic rather than acute.

4.5 Preventive Services Without Cost-Sharing

Recommended preventive services — including immunizations recommended by the Advisory Committee
on Immunization Practices and screenings with an A or B rating from the U.S. Preventive Services
Task Force — must be covered without any deductible, copayment, or coinsurance when delivered by
an in-network provider.
