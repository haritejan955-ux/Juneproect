# CMS Medicare Benefit Policy Manual

Source: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, Medicare Benefit Policy Manual (Pub. 100-02).
cms.gov. This file is a representative, educational summary of well-established public
provisions for use in a demo claims pipeline — it is not verbatim regulatory text and must not
be used as a substitute for the official manual or for legal/medical-billing advice.

1.1 Part A Inpatient Hospital Coverage

Medicare Part A covers medically necessary inpatient hospital services, including semi-private
room, meals, general nursing, and drugs furnished as part of inpatient treatment. Coverage for
each benefit period includes a deductible for the first 60 days, coinsurance for days 61-90, and
lifetime reserve days thereafter. A benefit period begins on admission and ends after 60
consecutive days without inpatient or skilled nursing care.

1.2 Skilled Nursing Facility Coverage

Following a qualifying inpatient hospital stay of at least 3 consecutive days, Part A covers up
to 100 days of skilled nursing facility (SNF) care per benefit period. The first 20 days are
covered in full; days 21-100 require daily coinsurance. Coverage requires that skilled nursing
or rehabilitation services be furnished on a daily basis and that, as a practical matter, the
services can only be provided in a SNF on an inpatient basis.

2.1 Part B Outpatient Therapy Services

Medicare Part B covers medically necessary outpatient physical therapy, occupational therapy,
and speech-language pathology services when furnished under a certified plan of care and
periodically reviewed by a physician or qualified non-physician practitioner. CPT codes commonly
billed for physical therapy include 97110 (therapeutic exercise), 97112 (neuromuscular
reeducation), and 97140 (manual therapy). Claims for therapy services above the annual threshold
amount require the KX modifier attesting that continued services are medically necessary and
documented in the beneficiary's record.

2.2 Durable Medical Equipment

Part B covers durable medical equipment (DME) that is medically necessary, able to withstand
repeated use, primarily and customarily used to serve a medical purpose, and appropriate for use
in the home. A physician's order and, for certain items, a certificate of medical necessity are
required before payment.

3.1 Preventive and Screening Services

Part B covers a defined set of preventive services without coinsurance or deductible when
furnished by a participating provider, including the annual wellness visit, specified cancer
screenings, cardiovascular disease screening, and diabetes screening, each subject to
frequency limits defined in the applicable National Coverage Determination.

3.2 Medical Necessity Standard

No payment may be made for items or services that are not reasonable and necessary for the
diagnosis or treatment of illness or injury, or to improve the functioning of a malformed body
member. A claim lacking documentation sufficient to establish medical necessity for the billed
procedure and diagnosis pairing may be denied on that basis alone, independent of any other
coverage determination.
